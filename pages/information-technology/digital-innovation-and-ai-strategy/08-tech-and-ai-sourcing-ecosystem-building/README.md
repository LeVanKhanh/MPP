# Module 08 — Tech and AI Sourcing & Ecosystem Building

> Pillar 1: Digital Innovation and AI Strategy · Priority: **P1** · Status: Not started

## Definition and scope

Deciding whether to build, buy or partner for technology and AI, and managing the vendor ecosystem after that decision.

- **In scope:** build–buy–partner, vendor due diligence, contracts and SLAs, data portability, lock-in, exit strategy, licensing, procurement risk.
- **Out of scope:** detailed cloud TCO (Module 14); technical supply-chain security (Module 15).

## Free learning resources

- [Martin Fowler — Utility vs Strategic Dichotomy](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/UtilityVsStrategicDichotomy.html) — telling software you should buy apart from software you should build.
- [Learn Wardley Mapping](https://learnwardleymapping.com/) — choosing build / buy / outsource by the maturity of each component.
- [NIST SP 800-161r1 — Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final) — a framework for managing vendor risk.

## Theory to master

- [ ] Have build – buy – partner criteria: strategic differentiation, time-to-value, 3-year TCO, switching cost.
- [ ] Know the contract terms that matter: SLA, data ownership, data portability, exit.
- [ ] Assess lock-in and the cost of leaving.
- [ ] Know the risks specific to buying AI solutions: input data, vendors changing models, per-token pricing.

## Practical deliverables

- **Deliverable:** a decision memo comparing build vs buy vs partner for an AI platform or an enterprise service.
- **Done when:** the recommendation rests on 3-year TCO, switching cost, security and time-to-value, and includes an exit option.
