# Module 15 — Cybersecurity: Risk Management, Governance and Strategy

> Pillar 2: Enterprise Transformation & Tech-Driven Agility · Priority: **P1** · Status: Not started

## Definition and scope

Cybersecurity seen from the leadership seat: which risks are acceptable, who is accountable, and how to present risk in terms of business damage.

- **In scope:** enterprise threat modelling, risk appetite, zero trust, IAM, supply chain security, cyber response, assurance, privacy.
- **Out of scope:** AI and data governance (Module 24); detailed attack and defence techniques.

## Free learning resources

- [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) — the most widely used cybersecurity governance framework, with the new Govern function.
- [NIST SP 800-30r1 — Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final) — how to assess likelihood and impact.
- [CISA — Resources & Tools](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools) — free guidance and tabletop exercises.
- [OWASP Top 10](https://owasp.org/projects/top-ten) — the most common classes of web application vulnerability.

## Theory to master

- [ ] Define risk appetite and residual risk, and who has the authority to accept residual risk.
- [ ] Understand the principles of zero trust and IAM.
- [ ] Know how to manage third-party and software supply chain risk.
- [ ] Know the incident response and escalation process.

## Practical deliverables

- **Deliverable:** a risk register of 10 risks: risk owner, likelihood / impact, controls, residual risk, incident escalation.
- **Done when:** every serious risk is expressed as business damage (money, reputation, disruption), not just as a list of CVEs.
