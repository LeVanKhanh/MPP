# Module 21 — Neural Networks and Applications

> Pillar 3: Leading AI, GenAI & Frontier Technologies · Priority: **P2** · Status: Not started

## Definition and scope

Understanding neural networks and foundation models deeply enough to challenge vendors and AI engineers. Coding deep learning yourself is not required.

- **In scope:** neural network concepts, foundation models, fine-tuning, RAG, hallucination, latency, model evaluation.
- **Out of scope:** enterprise-scale GenAI system architecture (Module 22).

## Free learning resources

- [3Blue1Brown — Neural Networks](https://www.3blue1brown.com/?topic=neural-networks) — the most visual video series on how neural networks and transformers work.
- [Neural Networks and Deep Learning — Michael Nielsen](http://neuralnetworksanddeeplearning.com/) — a free book that explains things from first principles.
- [Andrej Karpathy — Neural Networks: Zero to Hero](https://karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html) — build everything from scratch up to a small GPT, for those who want to go deep.
- [fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning](https://course.fast.ai/) — deep learning taught practice-first, theory second.
- [MIT 6.S191 — Introduction to Deep Learning](https://introtodeeplearning.com/) — MIT lectures, updated every year.

## Theory to master

- [ ] Explain how a neural network learns (forward pass, loss, backpropagation) at a conceptual level.
- [ ] Understand how foundation models, fine-tuning and RAG differ, and when to use each.
- [ ] Know the causes of hallucination and ways to reduce it.
- [ ] Know model evaluation metrics and the limits of benchmarks.

## Practical deliverables

- **Deliverable:** a table of limits and conditions of use for 3 kinds of solution: rules, supervised ML, LLM.
- **Done when:** you can use the table to challenge a vendor's AI proposal, with at least 3 technical questions they must answer.
