You Need

  • The story below, written large, and the six sentences to finish with one, two or three words.
  • For the buzz game: in many plurals the -s sounds like "z" (brothers, cousins, uncles, boys); after p, t and k it sounds like "s" (parents, aunts, cats).

A day at Grandma's

Last Sunday, my sister Daisy and I went to Grandma and Grandpa's house in the village. Dad took us in the car.

Grandma made a big chocolate cake, and Grandpa showed us his new puppy. We called him Buster! We played in the garden all afternoon.

In the evening, Uncle Jim came with our cousins, Zoe and Jack. We had dinner with them and we laughed a lot. It was the best day! – Charlie

1 Charlie and Daisy went to their grandparents' house last ____. · 2 They went there by ____. · 3 Grandma made a ____. · 4 Grandpa has got a new ____. · 5 The children played in the ____. · 6 Uncle Jim came with ____.

Answers: Sunday · car · (big) chocolate cake · puppy · garden · Zoe and Jack / their cousins.

Today's Lesson 30 min

  1. Hello (3 min): Hello! (Super Simple Songs).
  2. Watch (5 min): What did you do? (Past tense). Answer with something you did with your family.
  3. Read and write (7 min): read the story together. Your child writes one, two or three words for each sentence.
  4. Buzz like a bee (4 min): say family words with -s. When the end sounds like "z", your child buzzes "Zzzz!": brothers, cousins, uncles, sons, boys. When it sounds like "s" – parents, aunts, cats – stay quiet!
  5. My weekend (6 min): ask: "What did you do last weekend? Who did you see?" Your child answers, then writes two sentences: "On Saturday I went to the park with my dad. I played football."
  6. Watch (3 min): The Family Tree, the last time this week.
  7. Goodbye (2 min): Bye Bye Goodbye and a sticker for the week.

Say this: "We went to Grandma's house." … "Grandma made a big chocolate cake." … "What did you do last weekend?"

☐ Done

By the end of this week your child can…

  • Use the Movers family words – aunt, uncle, parent, grandparent, son, daughter, grandson, granddaughter – and explain a family tree.
  • Ask and answer "Who's the oldest / the loudest / the busiest in your family?"
  • Read a short story in the past and answer in one to three words, and say two things they did last weekend.
