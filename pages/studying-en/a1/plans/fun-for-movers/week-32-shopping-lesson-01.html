You Need

  • Your real kitchen, or the list "In our kitchen" below.
  • Paper for the shopping list and a pencil.
  • Use some with "There's / There are", and any with questions and "not": "There aren't any onions."

In our kitchen: lots of rice · only one carrot · some apples · six tomatoes · no onions · no cheese · lots of tea · no bread

For lunch with Grandma, we need: rice · three carrots · two onions · four tomatoes · some cheese · some bread · two apples. Tick (✓) the things we need to buy. Put a cross (✗) next to the things we've got.

Answers: Buy ✓: carrots, onions, cheese, bread. Don't buy ✗: rice, tomatoes, apples. Sentences, for example: There's lots of rice. There's only one carrot. There aren't any onions. Is there any bread? No, there isn't.

Today's Lesson 30 min

  1. Hello (3 min): Hello! (Super Simple Songs).
  2. Watch (5 min): Shopping Song for Kids. Say each food before the singer.
  3. Kitchen hunt (5 min): look in your kitchen together. Your child says: "There's some rice. There aren't any eggs." Kitchen words
  4. Make sentences (5 min): your child reads the list "In our kitchen" and makes sentences with some, lots of, only and any. Sentences
  5. What do we need? (6 min): your child compares the two lists and puts a tick or a cross next to each thing: "We need carrots – tick! We've got lots of rice – cross!"
  6. Shopping list (4 min): your child writes the shopping list for lunch with Grandma, then reads it to you: "We need three carrots and some bread."
  7. Goodbye (2 min): Bye Bye Goodbye.

Say this: "There's only one carrot." … "Are there any onions? – No, there aren't any." … "We need some bread."
