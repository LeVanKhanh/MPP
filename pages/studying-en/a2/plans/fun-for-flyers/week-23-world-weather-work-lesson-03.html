Week 23 · Lesson 3: A Windy Day Story

30 minutes for young learners (9–12). Lesson pages: Telling Stories and Numbers and ABC. Goes with Fun for Flyers – Unit 23: World, weather, work

Fun for Flyers · Week 23: World, weather, work | Lesson 3 of 3 | Level: A2 Flyers

Lesson 1 · World, weather, work words Lesson 2 · Ms Green's email Lesson 3 · A windy day story

You Need

  • Before the lesson, draw three pictures: 1 a windy street, a girl with a folder of homework papers · 2 the papers flying in the wind, a dog jumping · 3 the dog bringing a paper back, the girl and an old man laughing.
  • Cards with short forms: there's · I've · who's · we're · it's · don't · can't · they're.

Write about the story. Write 20 or more words. Start: It was a very windy day…

Two words or one word? there's = there is · I've = I have · who's = who is · we're = we are · it's = it is · don't = do not · can't = cannot · they're = they are

Answers: Story: any story of 20 or more words that matches the pictures. Short forms as shown.

Today's Lesson 30 min

  1. Hello (3 min): Hello! (Super Simple Songs).
  2. Tell the pictures (5 min): your child tells you what happens in each picture: "In the first picture, a girl is walking…"
  3. Write the story (10 min): your child writes the story with 20 words or more. Count the words together!
  4. Watch (3 min): Contractions! Say the long and short forms with the video.
  5. Two words or one? (5 min): your child matches each short form to its two words, then finds short forms in their own story.
  6. Read it (2 min): your child reads their story aloud with feeling.
  7. Goodbye (2 min): Bye Bye Goodbye and a sticker for the week.

Say this: "It was a very windy day." … "Suddenly the papers flew away!" … "There's a dog with my homework!"

☐ Done

By the end of this week your child can…

  • Read clues and find words about the world, weather and work: an office, a cave, a storm, a desert, a screen.
  • Add your own information to a business email so it makes sense.
  • Write a story of 20 words or more from three pictures, and use short forms: there's, I've, who's, we're.
