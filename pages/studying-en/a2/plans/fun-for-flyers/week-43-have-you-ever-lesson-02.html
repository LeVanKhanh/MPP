Week 43 · Lesson 2: Kites

You Need

  • The text below with three words next to each number.
  • Paper, sticks, string and tape if you want to make a small kite.

Kites: Have you ever (yet / ever / before) flown a kite? Kites aren't new. People have flown them (1 for / since / ago) more than two thousand years! The first kites were made (2 with / by / of) wood and paper. (3 Some / Any / Every) people used kites to send messages. About 200 years ago, people started (4 to use / using / use) big kites to find out about the weather. Today, kite festivals are very popular. At some festivals, the kites are so big that ten people have to (5 pull / pulling / pulled) them!

Answers: 1 for · 2 of · 3 Some · 4 using (to use is also fine) · 5 pull.

Today's Lesson 30 min

  1. Hello (3 min): Hello! (Super Simple Songs).
  2. Read and choose (9 min): read the text together. Your child chooses the right word for each number and says why.
  3. Questions (4 min): ask: "What were the first kites made of? What did people use kites for?"
  4. Make or draw a kite (9 min): your child makes a small kite (or draws one) and describes it: "My kite is made of paper. It's got a long tail."
  5. Have you ever…? (3 min): your child asks you three "Have you ever…?" questions about kites, festivals and windy days.
  6. Goodbye (2 min): Bye Bye Goodbye.

Say this: "People have flown kites for more than two thousand years!" … "The first kites were made of wood and paper." … "Have you ever been to a kite festival?"
