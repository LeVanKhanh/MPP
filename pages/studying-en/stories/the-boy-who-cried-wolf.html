1. The Story

A simple, gentle retelling of the traditional fable attributed to Aesop.

A quiet day on the hill

A young shepherd took his sheep to a grassy hill near his village. Every morning, he watched them eat. Every evening, he brought them home.

One afternoon, everything was quiet. The sheep were eating, and the boy had nobody to talk to.

“I am bored,” he said. “I want something exciting to happen.”

Then he had an idea. He stood on a rock and shouted towards the village.

“Wolf! Wolf! Please come and help!”

A trick that was not funny

The villagers heard his call. They left their work and hurried up the hill.

“Where is it?” a farmer asked. “Are the sheep safe?”

The boy laughed. “There is no wolf! I wanted to see you run!”

The villagers were angry. “Don’t tell lies about danger,” they said. “We came because we thought you needed help.”

They returned to the village, but the boy still thought his trick was funny.

The same lie again

A few days later, the boy felt bored again. He climbed onto the rock and called, “Wolf! Help me!”

Once more, the villagers ran to him. Once more, they found only the boy and his quiet sheep.

“You have tricked us again,” said the farmer. “How can we trust what you say?”

The boy watched them leave. This time, nobody smiled.

A real wolf appears

Later that week, a grey wolf came out from behind the trees. The sheep scattered across the hill.

The boy was frightened. “There is a real wolf!” he shouted. “Please help! I am telling the truth!”

Down in the village, the people heard him. But they remembered his tricks.

“He is trying to fool us again,” they said. Nobody went up the hill.

The wolf chased the sheep into the woods. When it was gone, the boy could not find some of them.

A lesson about trust

That evening, the farmer found the boy sitting beside the path.

“I am sorry I lied,” the boy said. “Today I really needed help.”

“Your lies made it hard for us to believe you,” the farmer replied.

The boy looked down. He understood that his words mattered. A trick that seemed funny had cost him other people’s trust.

Word note: In the title, cried means “called out loudly.” It does not mean “shed tears” here.

Singular and plural: one sheep → two sheep; one wolf → two wolves; one villager → two villagers.

4. Sentence Patterns to Learn

1. Describe a feeling

Pattern: Subject + am/is/are + feeling adjective.

Story example: “I am bored.”

Another example: “The villagers are angry.”

Try it: I am ______ because ______.

For the past, use was or were: “The boy was frightened.”

2. Say what you want to do

Pattern: Subject + want/wants + to + base verb.

Example: “I want to play a game.”

Try it: I want to ______ after school.

In the past: “The boy wanted to see the villagers run.”

3. Say that something exists

Pattern: There is + singular noun. / There are + plural noun.

Story example: “There is a real wolf!”

Negative example: “There is no wolf!”

Try it: There is a ______ near the ______.

Use there are for more than one: “There are five sheep.”

4. Ask someone to do something

Pattern: Please + base verb.

Story example: “Please come and help!”

Try it: Please ______ me.

Use a clear request when you need help: “Please help me find my teacher.”

5. Tell someone not to do something

Pattern: Don’t + base verb.

Story example: “Don’t tell lies about danger.”

Try it: Don’t ______ near the road.

Don’t means “do not.”

6. Explain a reason

Pattern: Result + because + reason.

Example: “The villagers were angry because the boy lied.”

Try it: The boy was frightened because ______.

Ask why to find a reason: “Why did the villagers stop believing him?”

Retell the story: First, the boy ______. Then, the villagers ______. Later, a real wolf ______. Finally, the boy learned ______.

5. Summary and Meaning

Story summary

A shepherd boy twice pretends that a wolf is near his sheep. The villagers run to help, but he laughs at them. When a real wolf arrives, they think he is lying again and do not come. Some sheep go missing, and the boy learns that his lies have damaged their trust.

The moral

When you keep telling lies, people may find it hard to believe you, even when you tell the truth.

Honesty helps build trust. The boy treats the villagers’ concern as a game. His false warnings waste their time and make his real warning harder to believe.

The lesson is to tell the truth, use calls for help honestly, and take responsibility when your actions hurt others. Trust can be rebuilt through honest behaviour over time.

Use the lesson in your life

  • When you make a mistake: Explain what happened truthfully instead of blaming someone else.
  • When you feel bored: Choose a harmless activity. Do not pretend that someone is in danger.
  • When you have told a lie: Correct it, apologise, and show through your actions that you are trying to be honest.
  • When you need help: Tell a trusted adult clearly what is happening. You should still ask for help, even if you have made mistakes before.

A thought for discussion: The villagers’ reaction explains the ending, but ignoring a possible danger can also cause harm. In real life, an adult can check what is happening while also helping a child learn to be honest.

6. Questions About the Story

Answer these questions about the written story above. You can speak, write, or draw your answers.

A. Understand the story

  1. What is the boy’s job?
  2. Where does he take the sheep?
  3. Why does he decide to play a trick?
  4. What does he shout to bring the villagers up the hill?
  5. How do the villagers feel when they discover the first trick?
  6. How many false warnings does the boy give before the real wolf appears?
  7. What do the sheep do when the real wolf appears?
  8. Why does nobody come when the boy really needs help?
  9. What has happened to some of the sheep by the time the wolf is gone?
  10. What does the boy learn about his words?

B. Put the events in order

Write the letters in the order in which the events happen.

  • A. A real wolf appears, and the sheep scatter.
  • B. The boy apologises to the farmer.
  • C. The bored boy gives his first false warning.
  • D. The villagers stay in the village when he calls again.
  • E. The boy tricks the villagers a second time.

C. Think and discuss

  1. Why is pretending that someone is in danger different from playing a harmless game?
  2. What could the boy do when he feels bored?
  3. What could the boy do to begin rebuilding the villagers’ trust?
  4. If you were a villager, how could you check whether the boy needed help?

D. Make it your own

Imagine that the boy meets a villager the next morning. Write or act out a short conversation. Include an apology, a reason why the lie was harmful, and one action the boy will take to make things better.

Answer: B. Put the events in order

C → E → A → D → B

Example responses: C. Think and discuss

Other thoughtful answers are welcome.

  1. A false warning makes people afraid and can distract them from real work or real danger. In a harmless game, everyone understands that it is a game.
  2. He could sing, count the sheep, or ask an adult for ideas for an activity that still lets him watch the flock.
  3. He could admit his lies, apologise, and show honesty consistently. Saying sorry is a beginning; rebuilding trust takes time.
  4. An adult could check the hill from a safe place or go with other adults to see what is happening.
Example conversation: D. Make it your own

Boy: “I am sorry I lied about the wolf. I frightened you and wasted your time.”

Villager: “Thank you for telling the truth now. What will you do differently?”

Boy: “I will not use danger as a joke. I will tell you honestly when I need help.”

Villager: “That is a good start. You can rebuild trust by being honest every day.”
