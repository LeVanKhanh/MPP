01 · ReadThe Story

A simple retelling of the traditional fable attributed to Aesop.

A very proud runner

In a green forest, a hare loved to talk about his long legs. “Look at me!” he said to the other animals. “I can run very fast. Nobody here can beat me!”

Nearby, a tortoise was walking along a path. The hare pointed at him and laughed. “Your legs are so short! You take such tiny steps!”

The tortoise stopped and looked at the hare. “You are faster than me,” he said, “but I will race you.”

“Race me?” cried the hare. “All right! Let’s race to the big tree.”

The race begins

The animals gathered beside the path. “Ready, set, go!” they called.

Away ran the hare. Soon, he was far ahead. The tortoise followed at his own pace, taking one small step after another.

The hare looked back. He could hardly see the tortoise. “This is too easy,” he thought. “I have plenty of time. I will take a nap.” He lay down in the shade and closed his eyes.

One step at a time

The tortoise reached the sleeping hare. He did not stop to laugh. He looked towards the big tree and kept walking.

“One more step,” he told himself. “I can do this.”

At last, the hare opened his eyes. The animals were cheering. The tortoise was only a few steps from the finish line!

The hare jumped up and raced along the path. But before he reached the tree, the tortoise crossed the finish line.

A surprising winner

“You won!” said the hare. “I thought being fast was enough.”

“I am slow,” replied the tortoise, “but I kept going.”

The hare lowered his ears. He had learned an important lesson: being good at something does not mean you can stop trying.

03 · Build your word bankVocabulary

Read each word, say it aloud, and use it in a new sentence.

Words and phrases from the story
Word or phraseSimple meaningExample
tortoise (noun)An animal with a hard shell that lives on land.The tortoise has short legs.
hare (noun)An animal like a rabbit, with long ears and strong back legs.The hare runs along the path.
proud (adjective)Very pleased with yourself; here, thinking you are better than others.The proud hare talks about his speed.
race (noun / verb)A competition to see who finishes first; to compete in one.They race to the big tree.
beat (verb)To win against someone in a game or competition.Can the tortoise beat the hare?
path (noun)A narrow way that people or animals walk along.The animals stand beside the path.
ahead (adverb)In front of someone.The hare is far ahead.
pace (noun)The speed at which someone moves.The tortoise walks at his own pace.
take a nap (phrase)To sleep for a short time.The hare decides to take a nap.
shade (noun)A place where something blocks direct sunlight.He rests in the shade of a tree.
keep going (phrase)To continue moving or trying.The tortoise does not stop. He keeps going.
cheer (verb)To shout to show happiness or support.The animals cheer for the runners.
finish line (noun)The line that marks the end of a race.The tortoise crosses the finish line.
win (verb)To come first or succeed in a competition.The tortoise wins the race.

Useful opposites: fast ↔ slow · ahead ↔ behind · win ↔ lose

Past-tense helpers: run → ran · say → said · take → took · keep → kept · win → won · think → thought

04 · Speak and writeSentence Patterns to Learn

1. Talk about ability

Subject + can + base verb.

Example: “I can run very fast.”

Try it: I can ______.

Use the base verb after can: “She can run.”

2. Compare two things

Subject + be + comparative adjective + than + someone/something.

Example: “You are faster than me.”

Try it: A ______ is slower than a ______.

Fast → faster; slow → slower.

3. Suggest an activity

Let’s + base verb.

Example: “Let’s race to the big tree.”

Try it: Let’s ______ together.

Let’s means “let us.”

4. Say what you will do

Subject + will + base verb.

Example: “I will race you.”

Try it: I will ______ tomorrow.

Use this pattern for a decision or promise.

5. Talk about continuing

Subject + keep/keeps/kept + verb-ing.

Example: “He kept walking.”

Try it: Even when it is hard, I keep ______.

Kept is the past form of keep.

6. Connect different ideas

Idea + but + different idea.

Example: “I am slow, but I kept going.”

Try it: This is difficult, but I can ______.

Use but to show a contrast.

Retell the story: First, the hare ______. Then, he ______. Meanwhile, the tortoise ______. Finally, the tortoise ______.

05 · Think about the lessonSummary and Meaning

Story summary

A hare is sure that he will beat a tortoise in a race. He runs ahead and stops to sleep. The tortoise continues walking and reaches the finish line first. The hare learns that speed alone is not enough.

The moral

“Slow and steady wins the race.”

The tortoise succeeds because he stays focused and continues trying. The hare loses because he is too sure of himself and stops before the job is done.

The lesson is to keep making progress, respect other people, and put effort into what you do. Being slow does not always make someone the winner. In this story, steady effort matters because the faster runner stops trying.

Use the lesson in your life

  • When learning English: Practise a few words regularly. Small steps can help you improve.
  • When something feels difficult: Break it into smaller steps and try the next step.
  • When you are good at something: Keep practising and be kind to people who are still learning.

06 · Check your understandingQuestions About the Story

Answer these questions about the written story above. You can speak, write, or draw your answers.

A. Understand the story

  1. Who are the two main characters?
  2. What does the hare like to talk about?
  3. Why does the hare laugh at the tortoise?
  4. Where do the animals agree to finish the race?
  5. Who is far ahead at the beginning?
  6. Why does the hare decide to take a nap?
  7. What does the tortoise do when he reaches the sleeping hare?
  8. What does the hare see when he opens his eyes?
  9. Who wins the race, and why?
  10. What important lesson does the hare learn?

B. Put the events in order

Write the letters in the order in which the events happen.

  • A. The tortoise crosses the finish line.
  • B. The hare stops to take a nap.
  • C. The tortoise says he will race the hare.
  • D. The hare wakes up and runs again.
  • E. The hare runs far ahead.

C. Think and discuss

  1. Was it kind of the hare to laugh at the tortoise? What could he say instead?
  2. How might the tortoise feel at the start and at the end of the race?
  3. What might happen if the hare ran the whole race without stopping?
  4. What is one thing you want to improve? What small step could you take today?

D. Make it your own

Imagine that the hare meets the tortoise the next day. Write or act out a short conversation. Include an apology and a kind reply.
