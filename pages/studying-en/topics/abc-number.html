Basic sentences Sentences

How many ___? – Five!

  • How many ducks?
  • One, two, three, four, five. Five ducks!
  • How many cars?
  • Four cars.

What number is it? – It's ___.

  • What number is it?
  • It's four.
  • What number is the bus?
  • It's number five!

How old are you? – I'm ___.

  • How old are you?
  • I'm four!
  • How old is your brother?
  • He's six.

What's this letter? / Can you spell ___?

  • What's this letter?
  • It's B.
  • Can you spell "dog"?
  • Yes! D, O, G.

💬 Let's talk

  • How old are you?
  • How many people are in your family?
  • Can you spell your name?

Parent's Corner

Game: Number jump

  • Write big numbers on sheets of paper and put them on the floor. "Jump to number four! … How many jumps? Five jumps!" Start with 1–5; add more numbers only when they are easy.
  • Saying numbers is not counting. Many young children can say "one, two, three…" quickly but do not yet link each number to one thing. Count slowly, touch one thing for each number, and say the last number again: "One, two, three. Three cars!"
  • Common mistake: "five duck" → "Five ducks!" Say the -s a little longer.
  • Letters: for very young children, the ABC song and the first letter of their own name are enough. Spelling words comes later.

Review Next Time

Ask these with toys and number cards. Holding up fingers is a good answer.

  1. (Put out four toys) How many?
  2. (Show a number card) What number is it?
  3. Count to five!
  4. How old are you?
  5. (Show the first letter of your child's name) What's this letter?
