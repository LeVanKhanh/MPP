Basic sentences Sentences

Do you like ___? – Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

  • Do you like cake?
  • Yes, I do!
  • Do you like sandwiches?
  • No, I don't.

I'm hungry! / I'm thirsty!

  • I'm hungry! Can I have a sandwich, please?
  • Here you are.
  • I'm thirsty! Can I have some water, please?
  • Yes, here you are.

I eat ___. / I drink ___.

  • I eat eggs for breakfast.
  • I drink milk.
  • We eat rice for dinner.
  • Is he hungry or thirsty?

They're good for you. / They're bad for you.

  • Fruit is good for you.
  • Water is good for you.
  • Vegetables are good for you.
  • Lots of sweets are bad for you.

Would you like some ___? – Yes, please. / No, thank you.

  • Would you like some more pasta?
  • Yes, please!
  • Would you like a cup of tea?
  • No, thank you.

First, ___. Then, ___. Last, ___.

  • First, put the butter and sugar in a bowl.
  • Then add an egg and mix them.
  • Next, add the flour.
  • Last, put the biscuits in the oven.

You make it with ___.

  • You make a sandwich with bread.
  • You make soup with vegetables and water.
  • You make a milkshake with milk and fruit.
  • You can put sauce on pasta.

Parent's Corner

Game: Café

  • Set the table with real or toy food. Your child is the waiter: "Are you hungry?" "Yes! Can I have a sandwich, please?" "Here you are!" Then swap.
  • About the words: sandwich, hungry, thirsty, vegetables and bad are Cambridge Movers words; crisps comes from preschool books. British English says crisps for the thin snack in a bag and chips for hot fried potatoes.
  • Common mistake: "I hungry." → "I'm hungry!" Just say it back with a smile.
  • Tip: talk in English at real snack times: "Are you hungry? Are you thirsty? Do you like it?"

Review Next Time

Ask these at snack time. Pointing or one word is a good answer.

  1. (Show a cup of water) What's this?
  2. Are you hungry or thirsty?
  3. Do you like cake?
  4. What do you eat for breakfast?
  5. Is fruit good for you?
