Basic sentences Sentences

Where's ___? – He's / She's in the ___.

  • Where's Mum?
  • She's in the kitchen.
  • Where's Teddy?
  • He's in the bedroom!

There's a ___ in the ___.

  • There's a bed in the bedroom.
  • There's a bath in the bathroom.
  • There's a sofa in the living room.
  • There are flowers in the garden.

How many ___? – There are ___.

  • How many chairs?
  • There are seven chairs.
  • How many windows?
  • Ten windows!

I ___ in the ___.

  • I sleep in my bedroom.
  • We eat in the kitchen.
  • We watch TV in the living room.
  • I play in the garden.

It's upstairs. / It's downstairs.

  • Where's your bedroom?
  • It's upstairs, next to the bathroom.
  • Is the kitchen upstairs?
  • No, it's downstairs.

In picture A ___, but in picture B ___.

  • In picture A the door is red, but in picture B it's green.
  • In picture A there's a cat on the roof, but in picture B there's a bird.
  • In picture A there are three windows, but in picture B there are four.
  • In picture A a man is on the balcony, but in picture B it's a woman.

Who lives above / below ___? – ___ lives ___.

  • Who lives above Grandma?
  • Mr Fox lives above her.
  • The Browns live below us. They're very quiet.
  • My friend lives opposite me.

We live on the ___ floor. / at the top of the stairs

  • We live on the third floor.
  • Her flat is at the top of the stairs.
  • The bikes are at the bottom of the stairs.
  • Is your flat near the lift?

💬 Let's talk

  • Do you live in a house or a flat?
  • Where do you sleep?
  • How many chairs are in your kitchen?
  • Who lives near you? Who lives above or below you?

Parent's Corner

Game: Where's Teddy?

  • Hide Teddy in a room while your child closes their eyes. "Where's Teddy? Is he in the kitchen?" Your child looks and answers: "He's in the bathroom!" Then your child hides Teddy and you ask.
  • Common mistake: "Teddy in bedroom." → "Teddy's in the bedroom!" Say the full sentence back with a smile.
  • Common mistake: "There is two chairs." → "There are two chairs!" Use there are for more than one.
  • Common mistake: "I live in the second floor." → "Oh, you live on the second floor!" We say on a floor, but at the top or bottom of the stairs.
  • Tip: do the lesson around your real home. Moving from room to room keeps young children busy and helps the words stick.

Review Next Time

Ask these around the house. Pointing or one word is a good answer.

  1. (Stand in the kitchen) What room is this?
  2. Where's your bed?
  3. Where's Mum? (Point to her room.)
  4. How many chairs are there?
  5. Where do you watch TV?
