Basic sentences Sentences

There's one ___. / There are two ___s.

  • There's one cat, and there are two dogs.
  • There's a box on the table.
  • There are three boxes under the bed.
  • I've got two watches!

One mouse, two mice!

  • There's a mouse. No, there are five mice!
  • One man and two women are at the bus stop.
  • Lots of children are playing in the park.
  • My feet are cold!

How many ___ are there? – There are ___.

  • How many sheep are there?
  • There are six sheep.
  • How many teeth have you got?
  • I don't know! Let's count them.

This is my ___. / These are my ___s.

  • This is my shoe. These are my shoes.
  • These shorts are blue.
  • Where are my glasses?
  • They're on your head!

💬 Let's talk

  • How many people are in your family? How many children?
  • How many toys are on your bed?
  • What do you wear on your feet?

Parent's Corner

Game: Plural quiz

  • Say a word for one: "A mouse!" Your child says many: "Two mice!" Then swap. One point for each right answer; special plurals get two points.
  • Common mistake: "Two mouses." → "Two mice!" Also "foots" → feet, "tooths" → teeth, "childs" → children, "mans" → men.
  • Common mistake: "Three sheeps." → "Three sheep!" Fish and sheep don't change. "My shorts is blue." → "Your shorts are blue!" (shorts, trousers, jeans and glasses always take are).
  • Tip: listen for the ending. After s, x, sh and ch we say an extra beat: box – box-es, watch – watch-es.
