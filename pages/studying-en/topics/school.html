Basic sentences Sentences

What's this? – It's a ___.

  • What's this?
  • It's a pencil.
  • What are these?
  • They're crayons.

What colour is it? – It's ___.

  • What colour is your bag?
  • It's red.
  • Is your book yellow?
  • Yes, it is!

Have you got a ___? – Yes, I have.

  • Have you got a crayon?
  • Yes, I have. Here you are.
  • Can I have a pencil, please?
  • Here you are. – Thank you!

Teacher says: ___ your ___.

  • Open your book.
  • Look at the board.
  • Listen to the teacher.
  • Colour the picture.

How do you come to school? – By ___. / I walk.

  • How do you come to school?
  • By bus.
  • Do you come by car?
  • No, I walk.

When do you have ___? – On ___.

  • When do you have English lessons?
  • On Monday and Thursday.
  • Where do you do your homework?
  • In the kitchen.

Parent's Corner

Game: What's in the bag?

  • Put a book, a crayon and a pencil in a bag. Your child closes their eyes, puts a hand in and feels one: "What's this? Is it a pencil?" Then it's your turn to guess.
  • Common mistake: "It's pencil." → "Yes! It's a pencil!" with a smile, and move on.
  • Common mistake: "Red bag it's." → "It's a red bag!" Very young children often put words in their own order; just say the sentence back the right way.
  • Tip: use your child's real school bag. Things they touch every day are the easiest words to remember.

Review Next Time

Ask these at the start of the next session with real school things. Pointing or one word is a good answer.

  1. (Show a book) What's this?
  2. (Show a red crayon) What colour is it?
  3. Have you got a pencil?
  4. Open your book! … Close your book!
  5. What's in your bag?
