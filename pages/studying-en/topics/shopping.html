Basic sentences Sentences

Can I have ___, please? – Here you are.

  • Can I have three apples, please?
  • Here you are.
  • I'd like some bread and some cheese, please.
  • Thank you! Goodbye!

There's some ___. / There are lots of ___.

  • There's some rice in the cupboard.
  • There are lots of oranges in the bowl.
  • There are only two tomatoes.
  • There's nothing in my bag!

Is there any ___? / Are there any ___?

  • Is there any milk?
  • Yes, there's some in the kitchen.
  • Are there any carrots?
  • No, there aren't any. Let's buy some!

We need ___. / We don't need ___.

  • We need some potatoes and some onions.
  • We don't need any pasta. We've got lots of it.
  • How many eggs do we need?
  • Six, please.

Parent's Corner

Game: Play shops

  • Put food from the kitchen on a table. Your child is the shopkeeper. You ask: "Can I have some rice, please? Are there any kiwis?" Your child answers and puts things in your bag. Then swap.
  • Common mistake: "Are there some carrots?" → "Are there any carrots?" We usually say any in questions and with not, and some in other sentences.
  • Common mistake: "I want buy apple." → "You want to buy some apples!" "Money", "sell", "empty", "full" and "much" (as in "not much") are Flyers words; "How much…?" is fine at Movers.

Review Next Time

Ask these at the start of the next session. Short answers are fine.

  1. Where can you buy fruit and vegetables?
  2. Is there any cheese in the kitchen?
  3. What do you say in a shop when you want bread?
  4. What do we need to buy this week?
  5. Name three things that are in your kitchen now.
